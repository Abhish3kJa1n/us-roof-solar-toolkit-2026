How the calculators work

This page describes the formulas and assumptions in the current build. It is not an engineering specification, utility-bill model, roof inspection, professional design or contractor quote.

Home / Calculation methodology

Input validation and editable assumptions

All calculator data-entry pages use inline field checks. Numeric fields show their allowed technical range, unit and entry increment; text fields enforce their stated format and length; paired fields check consistency. Hard-limit violations are identified inline and prevent calculation. Unusual but potentially valid values produce a non-blocking warning instead of an automatic rejection. The calculator reruns all relevant checks before producing a result. Source-loaded estimates remain editable and their provenance changes when the visitor changes them. The displayed ranges are technical guards for the model, not claims that every value inside them is typical or recommended.

1. Roof surface area

When you enter a measured roof-surface area, the calculator uses that figure directly and does not apply a second pitch adjustment. In the combined calculator's home-area route, the entered above-ground finished floor area is divided by the number of stories to approximate a building footprint. That footprint proxy is then adjusted by the selected pitch factor:

Pitch factor = √(1 + (rise ÷ run)²)

Examples: 2:12 ≈ 1.014; 4:12 ≈ 1.054; 6:12 ≈ 1.118; 9:12 = 1.250; 12:12 ≈ 1.414. A 1,600 sq ft footprint at 6:12 produces approximately 1,789 sq ft of sloped surface before overhangs, dormers, hips, valleys, attached structures or other geometric adjustments. The standalone roof-area calculator uses the selected exact pitch factor. Neither tool measures roof planes or complex geometry.

Dividing finished floor area by stories is only an approximation, not a measured footprint. Do not use it as a survey or order quantity. Measure separate roof planes, overhangs, porches, additions and complex features independently.

2. Roof replacement arithmetic

Estimated low/high cost = measured roof surface area × visitor-entered low/high installed price per roof sq ft.

The combined homeowner calculator preloads a broad Angi national roof-surface reference range of $4–$11 per sq ft when no approved local price record is available. This is deliberately labelled as national, not city-specific, not material-specific and not a contractor quote. The two values are editable, and visitors should replace them with comparable local proposal rates. The standalone roof-cost calculator also pre-fills the editable national reference; selecting Metal clears the generic range until the visitor selects an exact metal system, which then pre-fills a matching broad HomeGuide range. These references do not add automatic material premiums, pitch premiums, complexity multipliers, waste allowances or contingencies. The website cannot determine whether an entered rate is realistic or competitive.

The standalone roof-cost calculator's first material selector identifies the broad roof material. Selecting Metal reveals a second selector for one published price-reference category: either a roof style/system or a base-metal category. Selecting a category loads its matching editable HomeGuide reference range; the two category groups must not be combined. Changing the material clears a previously loaded publisher reference. Visitor-entered or edited rates are retained only with an explicit mixed/custom or visitor-entered explanation and are not relabelled as the newly selected category's published range. The calculation itself remains measured roof surface area multiplied by the low/high installed rates; the selectors do not add automatic multipliers. The calculator does not diagnose roof condition or decide whether repair or replacement is appropriate.

National survey context — not a calculator input

The U.S. Census Bureau and HUD's 2023 American Housing Survey reported a median roofing expenditure of $10,000 for the 2021–2023 reference period. The same infographic reports approximately 8.3 million roofing projects and $93.5 billion in total roofing expenditure. These are national owner-reported home-improvement statistics, not contractor invoice prices, current-year quotes, local prices or dollars per roof square foot. The underlying survey includes varied roof improvement scopes and does not establish that every project was a complete tear-off and replacement. Read the Census/HUD 2023 Home Improvements Infographic (PDF).

Arithmetic example, not a market benchmark: 1,800 sq ft × $5.50–$8.00 per sq ft = $9,900–$14,400. A roofing square is 100 sq ft; do not confuse dollars per roofing square with dollars per sq ft.

3. Geographic installed-price references (separate from calculator inputs)

The site does not infer installation prices from a city name, electricity rate or PV production model. A solar installed-price reference may be published only from a reviewed residential rooftop PV cohort with current commercial reuse clearance. City samples require at least 100 projects; state fallback samples require at least 500. If displayed, the middle 50% (25th–75th percentiles), median, project count, installation years, nominal pre-incentive $/W DC basis, filters and source are shown. These are historical project-price distributions, not a current installer quote or a guaranteed price.

A roofing reference may be published only for a supported 2013 OMB CBSA metro after the official 2023 AHS relational PUF has been checked, the roofing job code verified, weighted estimates and replicate variance reviewed, and the results reconciled with Census Table C-16. The AHS measure is a whole-project nominal-dollar spending distribution, not a cost per square foot. It includes broader roof addition/replacement scopes and Census-edited/imputed values; it must not be used as the installed-rate input in the roof calculator. A selected current city is not automatically assigned a metro reference.

Current release status: no solar installed-price rows or AHS metro roofing rows have passed the publication gates. The tools therefore withhold geographic price distributions. The combined calculator uses a separately sourced national EnergySage H1 2026 median of $2.62/W as an editable fallback when no approved city/state LBNL row is available; this is marketplace context, not a city price or contractor quote. The national AHS $10,000 roofing figure is historical survey context only, not a current local price or calculator input. Only reference data that meets the stated publication criteria is displayed. Until a qualifying geographic distribution is available, the tools use the separately sourced national references described above.

4. Solar system size and production

Target annual generation = annual household electricity use (kWh) × visitor-entered target percentage.

Approximate DC system size (kW) = target annual generation (kWh) ÷ visitor-entered annual production per DC kW (kWh/kW/year).

Estimated annual generation = estimated DC system size × visitor-entered annual production per DC kW.

Both solar calculators require annual household consumption in kWh from utility bills. They do not estimate consumption by dividing total bill dollars by a retail rate; fixed customer charges, demand charges, taxes and other components can distort that shortcut.

The separate city solar production estimator can request a location-specific typical-year model from the NLR PVWatts V8 service using a selected US Census representative place point and editable array assumptions. Its annual kWh per kW DC value is modeled annual AC energy divided by modeled DC capacity. When the visitor returns to the combined calculator, the modeled yield and selected city/state are prefilled and editable. A separate EIA state-average benchmark may also be prefilled as an explicitly provisional scenario rate; it is not the household tariff. An approved LBNL city/state historical solar price record may replace the national solar-price fallback only after every publication gate has passed; no such record is currently approved. The model does not inspect a roof, determine shading, validate structural capacity or establish that the proposed system physically fits. Actual usage, self-use share, export compensation and property-specific quotes remain household-specific inputs.

The accepted 100–4,000 kWh/kW/year input range is a broad typo check only. It is not a U.S. benchmark or recommended production range.

5. Annual solar energy-flow scenario

No household direct-use share, actual marginal import tariff or export credit is inferred from city selection. A city-estimator handoff may prefill the EIA state-average retail price only as a clearly labelled, editable scenario input; it is not an actual household tariff and must be checked against the bill. A blank field means unknown; it is not silently treated as zero. An explicit $0 rate is accepted only where the applicable tariff supports it.

Self-consumed kWh = min(annual solar generation × visitor-entered annual direct-use share, annual household kWh).

Exported kWh = max(annual solar generation − self-consumed kWh, 0).

Modeled annual energy value = (self-consumed kWh × marginal import rate) + (exported kWh × utility export credit).

The import rate is needed only when modeled direct-use kWh are positive. The export credit is needed only when modeled exports are positive. If all modeled generation is used directly, an export credit is not needed. If no direct-use share is entered, the calculators do not split generation into direct use and exports or calculate total energy value. If a required tariff input is unknown, total annual dollar value and simple payback are withheld.

The visitor-entered annual direct-use share is a scenario assumption, not a measured hourly load profile. Actual self-consumption depends on when the household uses electricity and when the array produces it. This annual-share calculator does not simulate hourly or interval-meter data, seasonal variation, time-of-use periods, fixed charges, demand charges, minimum bills, taxes, battery dispatch, export caps, curtailment or utility-specific monthly credit rules.

6. Simple cash payback and financing illustration

Modeled cash system price = system DC watts × visitor-entered installed dollars per DC watt.

Simple cash payback = modeled cash system price ÷ first-year modeled annual energy value.

This is an undiscounted first-year arithmetic ratio, not ROI, NPV, IRR, a forecast or a financing recommendation. It does not include degradation, maintenance, inverter replacement, insurance, taxes, incentives, financing fees, future rate changes or the time value of money. The combined calculator's optional fixed-rate loan payment uses a standard monthly amortization formula from the entered principal, APR and term. It excludes dealer fees, origination charges, escalators, liens and lender underwriting; no loan approval or offer is implied.

7. Solar panel count

Panel count = ceiling(target DC system watts ÷ visitor-entered module nameplate watts).

If module area is entered, total module surface area is panel count multiplied by that area. The result excludes spacing, setbacks, pathways, fire access, obstructions, shade, roof-plane geometry, attachment design and structural constraints. It is not a roof-fit plan.

8. Worked arithmetic examples

  • Solar scenario: 10,800 kWh annual use, 80% target generation, 1,200 kWh/kW/year visitor-supplied production, 60% visitor-supplied annual direct-use share, $0.18/kWh marginal import rate and $0.04/kWh export credit. Target generation = 8,640 kWh; system size = 7.2 kW DC; direct use = 5,184 kWh; exports = 3,456 kWh. Modeled annual energy value = (5,184 × $0.18) + (3,456 × $0.04) = $1,071.36. At a visitor-entered $3/W installed cash price, modeled price = $21,600 and simple payback = 20.2 years. These are deliberately chosen example inputs, not typical or recommended assumptions.
  • Roof cost: 1,800 sq ft × $5.50–$8.00 per sq ft = $9,900–$14,400. No additional factor is applied.
  • Roof area: a 1,600 sq ft assumed footprint at 6:12 pitch = approximately 1,789 sq ft before unmodeled geometry.
  • Panel count: an 8 kW DC target with 400 W modules requires 20 modules; at 22 sq ft per module, module surface area totals 440 sq ft before spacing and access.

9. Sources and verification limits

  • NLR PVWatts — a visitor-directed planning model with uncertainty and site-input limitations.
  • U.S. Department of Energy: Evaluating Your Utility Rate Options — explains energy, demand and fixed-charge differences.
  • U.S. Department of Energy: Homeowner's Guide to Going Solar — roof suitability and project-planning information. Its live financing section includes historical federal tax-credit figures; do not use that section to determine current federal tax eligibility.
  • IRS 2025 law-change FAQ — current Section 25D homeowner-credit timing information as checked October 31, 2026. This page does not determine individual eligibility.
  • National Roofing Contractors Association homeowner resources — roof service-life context and project-scope guidance.
  • U.S. Census Bureau / HUD, 2023 Home Improvements Infographic — national owner-reported roofing expenditure context for 2021–2023; not a local quote dataset.

None of these sources validates a visitor's particular roof, electricity tariff, load profile, solar proposal, financing contract or tax position.

Editable starting references

The standalone roof replacement calculator pre-fills an editable national Angi range of $4–$11 per roof-surface square foot. Selecting Metal clears that general reference; selecting an exact metal system loads the corresponding published HomeGuide range. These are broad publisher references, not city prices or project quotes.

The standalone solar savings calculator pre-fills an editable $2.62 per DC watt EnergySage H1 2026 national solar-only marketplace median. If the selected city/state has an approved Berkeley Lab installed-price distribution in the public registry, the calculator independently verifies that record before replacing the national fallback. Otherwise the national reference remains. Visitor edits are never overwritten by a late-arriving benchmark lookup.

City + state PVWatts handoffs carry modeled annual production and location context. State-average EIA electricity prices may be shown as editable scenario context, not as a confirmed household marginal tariff. Annual usage, direct-use share and export compensation are still household-specific and remain for the visitor to supply.
