What these numbers do—and do not—tell you

  • They do: compare average revenue per kWh of residential electricity sold across states for the same eight-month period.
  • They do not: identify your utility’s current rate, marginal energy charge, time-of-use price, fixed monthly fee, demand charge, solar export credit, or net-metering policy.
  • They are not a solar-savings forecast: solar value depends on the exact tariff and when energy is produced, consumed and exported.

How the average is calculated

EIA calculates average retail price by dividing utility revenue from retail electricity sales by retail electricity sales in kilowatt-hours. The result includes delivered-electricity costs such as generation, transmission, distribution, taxes and fees. EIA distinguishes this average price from a utility rate, which applies to particular services and may vary by customer class, usage and time of use.

To estimate a solar project, use your current utility tariff and actual 12-month kWh history. Identify the marginal energy charge that one fewer imported kWh would avoid, and separately verify the export compensation. Do not assume that the statewide average equals either value.
