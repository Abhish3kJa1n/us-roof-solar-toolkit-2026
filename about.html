About Roof & Solar Toolkit

Free planning calculators and practical guidance for U.S. homeowners considering roof work or residential solar.

Home / About

Plan with clearer numbers

Roof & Solar Toolkit helps homeowners explore roof measurements, replacement costs and residential solar scenarios before comparing local proposals. The calculators turn stated inputs and identified reference values into planning estimates. The guides explain the decisions, costs and questions that commonly shape a project.

What the tools can tell you

Use the calculators to test assumptions, compare scenarios and see which inputs have the greatest effect on an estimate. Each result should be read alongside the assumptions and limitations shown with the tool.

The tools do not inspect a property, measure a roof, design a solar system, identify your utility tariff, confirm permit requirements or provide a contractor quote. Actual project costs and savings depend on property conditions, local labor and materials, system design, utility rules, financing and other details that require current local information.

How the estimates are grounded

Solar-production estimates use NLR PVWatts V8 with representative place information from U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb. Utility-rate lookups use nearby records from OpenEI's Utility Rate Database, which must be checked against the household's actual tariff. Public EIA data provide broad electricity-price context. Published 2026 EnergySage and Angi figures may be shown as optional national market references, not local installer quotes.

Roof and solar decisions can involve substantial cost. The tools identify their inputs and limits and leave unknown household rates blank rather than presenting a national average as a guaranteed saving. They do not replace a roof inspection, structural assessment, utility confirmation, tax advice or written local proposal.

Sources and geographic coverage

Calculators and guides draw on government data, technical modeling resources, utility information and published market research where suitable sources are available. A source may provide background context without being used directly in a calculation.

National, state, metropolitan and city-level figures describe different things. Pages identify the relevant geography, period, units and limitations. Where the available evidence does not support a reliable local estimate, the site may withhold that estimate rather than imply a level of precision the data cannot support.

See Calculation Methodology for formulas, assumptions and source references. The Editorial Policy explains how information is selected, updated and corrected.

Use estimates as a starting point

  • Check that your measurements, rates and units match the documents you have.
  • Use current written local proposals when comparing project costs.
  • Confirm utility rates and export rules with your serving utility.
  • Use qualified professionals for inspections, structural assessments, system design and code requirements.
