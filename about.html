About Roof & Solar Toolkit

Free planning calculators and practical explanations for U.S. homeowners considering roof work or residential solar.

What this resource is for

Roof & Solar Toolkit is an independently operated U.S. homeowner planning publication maintained by a solo publisher under the Roof & Solar Toolkit name. The publisher is responsible for the site's research, calculator maintenance, source documentation and corrections. The publication is intended to help homeowners understand early-stage roof-area, roof-replacement and residential-solar scenarios before comparing local proposals or speaking with qualified professionals.

The tools are designed for use in the United States. The publication is not a roofing contractor, solar installer, utility, government agency, inspection service, lender or tax adviser, and is not affiliated with those organizations.

What the tools do — and do not do

Calculators apply visible formulas to visitor inputs and, where explicitly stated, selected source-based reference values. They can help explore arithmetic scenarios and understand which assumptions drive a result. They do not inspect a property, measure a roof, identify a household's utility tariff, design a solar system, confirm permit requirements, provide a contractor quote or determine tax-credit eligibility.

Results can differ materially from an actual project because of roof geometry, material and labor scope, access, local code, structural condition, shading, orientation, hourly electricity use, utility export rules, financing terms and other property-specific factors. Obtain measurements, current local proposals and applicable utility or government information before making a financial or construction decision.

Publisher responsibility

The publication does not present a separate editorial team, professional reviewer or credentialed technical staff. Its publisher identity describes who is responsible for maintaining this resource; it is not a claim of roofing, electrical, engineering, legal or tax qualifications. Any future independent review will identify the actual reviewer, their relevant qualifications and the specific material reviewed.

Sources and geographic limits

Where suitable information is available, the site refers to government datasets, technical modeling resources, utility information and published market research. A source may provide context without being used as a calculator input. National, state, metropolitan and city-level figures are not interchangeable; the page describing a figure should identify its geographic level, period, unit and limitations.

Some geographic price estimates are deliberately withheld because their sample, geographic-matching, reuse or quality checks have not met the site's publication criteria. A location selection does not by itself establish a local roof price, solar installation price or household electricity rate. See Calculation Methodology for the current formulas, references and publication status.

How to assess a result

  • Read the assumptions beside the calculator and check whether the units match your source documents.
  • Replace broad reference values with comparable written local rates or your actual utility-bill figures where the tool allows it.
  • Review the calculation method and linked source before relying on a benchmark.
  • Treat missing or unknown information as unknown; do not assume a blank field means zero unless the calculator explicitly says so.
  • Use qualified local professionals for property inspection, engineering, system design, code compliance and project quotes.

Accountability

The site publishes its calculation approach and source limitations so readers can examine how estimates are produced. It does not claim professional certification or independent technical review where none is identified. If a defined part of a calculator or article is independently reviewed in the future, the scope and reviewer will be described accurately.
