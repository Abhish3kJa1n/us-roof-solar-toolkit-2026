Why three bids can differ even when the roof looks the same

A homeowner discussion in southwest Ohio described three tear-off bids of $11,000, $16,000 and $23,000 for a 1,600-square-foot ranch. The homeowner said the lowest bidder would not itemize the scope. This is one anecdote, not a local price survey or a fair-price benchmark. Its useful lesson is narrower: a headline total cannot tell you whether the proposals include the same work. For each allowance, write down the quantity included and the unit rate beyond it. For concealed damage, agree on how the contractor will document the condition, obtain your approval and record the change order before extra work proceeds. Do not assume a percentage allowance is a fixed-price promise.

4. Resolve these points before signing

  • Verify contractor licensing where required, insurance and any local registration directly with the relevant authority or insurer.
  • Ask who obtains permits and schedules inspections, and confirm local requirements independently.
  • Get product names, installation details, exclusions, cleanup and warranty terms in the signed contract or an attached scope sheet.
  • Understand the deposit, payment milestones, cancellation terms and how additional work is approved and priced.
  • Ask each bidder to explain any unusually low price or missing scope item in writing.

How to use the result

A bid with a lower total may still leave more work unpriced. First resolve missing scope and unclear allowances; then compare the proposals on the work that is actually specified. A contractor's inspection and written proposal—not this worksheet—must determine what the roof needs.

The Federal Trade Commission's home-improvement guidance recommends multiple written estimates, checking licensing and insurance, and getting scope, materials, dates and price in a written contract. Local licensing, permit and insurance requirements vary; check the relevant local authority and insurer.
