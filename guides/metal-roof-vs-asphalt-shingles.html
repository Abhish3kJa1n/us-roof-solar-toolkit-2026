Metal Roof vs. Asphalt Shingles: Cost, Lifespan and Trade-Offs

If the budget is tight, asphalt shingles usually win on the first invoice. Metal can last longer, but that label covers everything from basic exposed-fastener steel panels to expensive standing seam and architectural systems. The right comparison is not “shingles versus metal.” It is this shingle product against this metal system, installed on this roof, with the same tear-off and flashing scope.

Quick comparison

For most homeowners, the first decision is cash available now versus the roof system they want to own for the long haul. Asphalt is familiar, widely installed and usually cheaper upfront. Metal may offer a longer service-life range, but the result depends on the exact panel or shingle system, metal, coating, fasteners and the crew installing it. Neither material gets a free pass on workmanship.

“Metal” is not a specification

A contractor saying “metal roof” has told you almost nothing. It could be corrugated steel with exposed screws, standing seam with concealed clips, metal shingles or a premium architectural system. Steel, aluminum, zinc and copper are different materials. Gauge, coating, panel width, seam design and trim change both price and installation requirements.

Make the proposal name the manufacturer, product line, base metal, thickness or gauge, finish, panel profile, fastening method and underlayment. If one contractor writes “26-gauge standing seam” and another writes “metal roofing,” those are not comparable bids. Do not sign until the vague quote gets specific.

How long do the two roof types last?

HUD's 2015 Durability by Design guide lists estimated service life of 15–30 years for composition shingles and 20–50+ years for standing-seam metal. It lists 50+ years for concrete/clay tile and 50–100 years for slate. The figures draw on industry data and a 2007 National Association of Home Builders / Bank of America component-life study; HUD cautions that estimates vary significantly within a material category and actual installed performance is uncertain.

The National Roofing Contractors Association gives a broad consumer reference that most new roofs are designed to provide useful service for about 20 years, while some types such as slate, clay tile and certain metals can last longer. The Metal Roofing Alliance, a trade association representing metal roofing interests, publishes a longer metal-roof life comparison. We do not blend these estimates: product-specific documentation, design, workmanship, climate, roof pitch, drainage and maintenance determine the actual outcome.

A “30-year” or “lifetime” label is warranty language, not a promise that the roof will perform for that many years without trouble. Read the exclusions, prorating, finish coverage, transfer rules and maintenance requirements. Then read the installer's separate workmanship warranty. A strong material warranty does not fix a bad flashing detail or a leaking fastener.

What actually drives the quote?

  • Material and product: a steel panel and a copper architectural roof are not remotely the same purchase.
  • Fastening system: concealed clips and standing seams take different parts and labor from exposed-fastener panels.
  • Roof complexity: steep pitch, hips, valleys, dormers, skylights and chimney transitions mean more cuts and flashing work.
  • Tear-off: ask how many existing layers are included and what the extra charge is if the crew finds more.
  • Decking: get the unit price for replacing damaged sheathing. “As needed” without a price is not a complete estimate.
  • Water details: underlayment, drip edge, valley metal, wall flashing, pipe boots and ridge details should be listed.
  • Permits and access: clarify who handles permits, disposal, site protection and difficult access equipment.

Do not buy a metal roof on a cooling-bill promise

Some finishes are made to reflect more solar radiation. That can reduce roof heat gain in the right climate and roof assembly. It does not mean every metal roof cuts every homeowner's cooling bill by a fixed percentage. Color, coating, insulation, attic ventilation, HVAC and local weather all matter. Ask for the actual product's reflectance and emittance data. Treat a generic “save 20% on cooling” pitch as marketing until someone shows how that number applies to your house.

Compare proposals like-for-like

NRCA recommends obtaining several detailed proposals and says a written proposal should identify the covering, manufacturer, materials, scope, removal/replacement, flashing, ventilation, installation method, schedule, payment procedures and warranties. Use this checklist before comparing the totals:

  1. Roof area and design: confirm the measured roof surface, pitch, complexity and waste assumptions are consistent.
  2. Exact product: identify shingle brand/line or metal alloy, gauge, profile, coating and fastener system.
  3. Removal: state whether old roofing is removed, how many layers are included and how disposal is priced.
  4. Water-management details: specify underlayment, ice/water barrier where required, drip edge, valleys and all flashing/penetration work.
  5. Decking: identify the included allowance and unit price for additional damaged sheathing.
  6. Ventilation and code: show the planned changes and permit/code responsibilities.
  7. Warranty: separate product coverage from labor/workmanship coverage, with exclusions and transfer terms.
  8. Project controls: confirm cleanup, protection, payment schedule, start/completion window and written change-order process.

NRCA's guidance is sensible here: compare the written scope, materials and workmanship, not just the total. The cheapest bid may have left out tear-off, flashing or deck repairs. The most expensive bid may simply have a bigger margin. Make every roofer explain the same line items before you decide.

What real homeowner discussions reveal

Recent homeowner discussions show why a one-line comparison is inadequate. In one October 2026 thread, a homeowner said a standing-seam proposal was about 60% more than architectural shingles; replies debated service life, insurance and the distinction between concealed and exposed fasteners. In another April 2026 discussion, a metal roof quote covering both a house and a garage was lower than a shingle-only quote for the house, prompting questions about tear-off, panel type and gauge. These are individual experiences, not representative pricing data, but they illustrate the need to compare exact scope and specifications.

When asphalt is the sensible call

If the upfront budget is tight, you may not plan to stay in the house for decades, or the existing roof is already built around a conventional shingle system, asphalt can be the practical choice. That does not mean buying the cheapest bundle on the shelf. Compare shingle line, underlayment, ventilation, flashing, tear-off and workmanship. A bargain roof that needs an early repair is no bargain.

When metal deserves a serious look

Metal is worth pricing when you expect to own the home for a long time, can handle the higher initial cost and have a contractor who regularly installs the exact system being proposed. Compare the extra upfront spend with realistic service-life expectations, maintenance, future replacement timing and warranty exclusions. If the installer mostly does asphalt and is treating your standing-seam roof as a side project, keep looking.

Frequently asked questions

Is a metal roof always more expensive than asphalt shingles?

Usually the broad published ranges put metal above common asphalt shingles, but the ranges overlap and “metal” includes many systems and alloys. Low-cost metal panels, premium asphalt products, roof geometry and scope can change the comparison. Compare current local proposals for the same roof.

Is a metal roof cheaper over its lifetime?

It can be competitive for some owners, but there is no universal answer. A simple installed-cost-per-estimated-year calculation leaves out the time value of money, repairs, coatings, energy effects, insurance, resale, future labor and the uncertainty of service-life estimates. Do not treat it as a complete life-cycle-cost analysis.

Can metal roofing be installed over existing shingles?

Some systems and jurisdictions permit certain recover installations, but this is not a blanket rule. The existing roof's condition, number of layers, deck, local code, weight, manufacturer instructions and warranty conditions must be checked by the installer and local authority. Concealing an existing moisture or deck problem is not a substitute for inspecting it.

Do metal roofs reduce cooling bills?

Some reflective metal roof finishes can reduce solar heat gain. Actual energy savings depend on the finish, color, climate, insulation, attic ventilation, HVAC and existing roof assembly. Ask for product-specific reflectance/emittance data and avoid generic savings promises.

Which is better before installing solar panels?

Neither material automatically wins. Confirm that the roof is suitable for the planned solar system, identify approved attachment and flashing details for the exact roofing product, and get written confirmation of how installation affects both roof and solar warranties. If the roof is near replacement, compare roof-before-solar sequencing first.

Pro Tip Here is the real-world catch: contractors can make two bids look comparable by using the same phrase, “metal roof,” while specifying different gauges, panel profiles, underlayments and fastening systems. One quote may also exclude tear-off or price decking repairs only after demolition. Put the exact product and those exclusions side by side. If a contractor refuses to state a unit price for likely extras, you are being asked to approve a blank cheque.
