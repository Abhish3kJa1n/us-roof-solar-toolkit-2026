Roof Replacement Cost in 2026: Average Price and Cost per Square Foot

A new roof price depends on the actual roof surface area, roofing system, pitch, tear-off, access, labor market and concealed repairs. This guide compares two current cost-publisher datasets, explains why they differ and shows how to turn a measured area and comparable written rates into a transparent planning scenario.

What does roof replacement cost per square foot mean?

Roofing is commonly priced by the roofing square: one roofing square equals 100 square feet of roof surface. A quote expressed per square foot should state the measurement basis and what is included in the rate.

Home floor area is not roof surface area. The actual roof can be larger because of pitch, overhangs, porches, garages, dormers and multiple roof planes. A 2,000-square-foot house does not necessarily have a 2,000-square-foot roof. A roof-area measurement is the better starting point.

For illustration only, applying Angi's broad $4–$11 rate to a measured 2,000-square-foot roof surface gives $8,000–$22,000 by multiplication. Applying HomeGuide's separate $3–$6 general rate gives $6,000–$12,000. These are arithmetic examples based on two publishers' published ranges—not predictions, a local quote or a statement that either rate includes identical work.

Use the roof area calculator to understand the measurement and its limitations. It uses a geometric pitch factor for a simplified footprint route; it does not replace a roof measurement, aerial report or contractor takeoff.

What makes a new roof cost more?

  • Actual roof area: measure the sloped surface, not the home's advertised living area. Pitch, overhangs and geometry change the amount of roof covering and underlayment required.
  • Roof pitch and access: steepness, height, limited staging space and complex geometry can increase labor, safety requirements and material waste.
  • Material and complete system: covering, underlayment, ice-and-water protection where required, starter, ridge, flashing, fasteners, trim and ventilation are distinct components.
  • Tear-off and disposal: number of existing layers, material weight, disposal charges, dumpster access and any permitted recover/overlay option affect the scope.
  • Decking and concealed conditions: rotted or damaged sheathing, framing, wet insulation and interior water damage can add cost after removal. The proposal should define unit rates and an approval process for extra work.
  • Roof penetrations and details: chimneys, skylights, valleys, walls, pipe boots, eaves and gutters require correctly specified flashing and transitions.
  • Location and season: local labor, permit requirements, material availability, weather and storm-related demand affect price. National averages do not provide reliable metro quotes.
  • Warranty and contractor scope: product, installation method, workmanship coverage, exclusions, cleanup and payment terms should be written down.

Angi's October 2026 cost guide separately calls out permits, old-roof removal, dumpster rental, post-installation inspections and warranties. HomeGuide's guide separates removal, material and labor in its per-roofing-square breakdown. Those component lines are useful prompts, but the homeowner should compare the final written scope rather than assume every quote includes the same items.

What should a roof replacement quote include?

Ask every contractor to price the same defined project. The total is difficult to compare until the roof measurement, product and work scope are clear.

Recent homeowner discussions show that quoted totals can differ substantially when one proposal reuses flashing and another replaces a broader set of components, or when roof area and underlayment are not specified. These discussions are anecdotal, not representative pricing evidence, but they illustrate why a lower total is not automatically comparable.

Calculate a roof replacement scenario

The roof replacement cost calculator multiplies a measured roof surface area by the low and high installed rates you enter. It does not estimate the condition of the roof, measure the property, determine which material is suitable or tell you whether a contractor's bid is fair.

The calculator includes an optional broad Angi reference range that must be loaded deliberately. If you have written local proposals, use rates that already reflect comparable material, labor, removal, disposal, underlayment, flashing, decking, access, permits and warranty scope. Do not mix a repair quote with a full replacement rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a new roof cost on average?

Angi reports a $9,610 national average and a $5,903–$13,378 most-project range in its October 2026 guide. HomeGuide reports a $10,800 national average and $5,700–$16,000 average range in its November 2025 guide. The sources use different data and are not one combined estimate.

What is the average roof replacement cost per square foot?

Angi reports a broad $4–$11 per square foot installed range. HomeGuide's general roof-replacement summary reports $3–$6 per square foot, while its material-specific figures differ. Confirm roof surface measurement and included scope before using any rate.

Does a 2,000-square-foot house need a 2,000-square-foot roof estimate?

No. Home floor area and roof surface area are different measurements. Pitch, overhangs, attached structures and roof geometry change the actual surface. HomeGuide's size table, for example, uses a 6:12 pitched roof and lists a 2,000-square-foot house with a larger estimated roof surface. A measured roof report is preferable.

Does roof replacement cost include tear-off?

Not automatically. Some cost summaries include removal in their installed rate; proposals may itemize it separately. Ask how many existing layers are included, how disposal is handled and whether the price changes if concealed conditions are found.

Is metal roofing always more expensive than asphalt?

Many metal systems have higher published installed rates than common asphalt shingles, but “metal” includes different materials and panel types. Compare the actual product and scope. See the dedicated metal roof cost guide.
