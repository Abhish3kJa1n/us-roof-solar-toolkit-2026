Solar Panel Warranties Explained: What the 25-Year Promise Actually Covers

A solar proposal can advertise a 25-year warranty without making clear which parts are covered, who pays for the work or what happens if the installer disappears. The panel warranty is only one part of the deal. Read the coverage, exclusions and claim process before treating the headline number as protection.

The main solar warranties are not interchangeable

Residential solar proposals often bundle several promises under one broad phrase such as “25-year warranty.” That can make very different protections sound like one product. Separate the documents and identify who is responsible for each one.

Do not assume every system includes every category, or that every warranty lasts the same number of years. Coverage depends on the exact equipment, contract, manufacturer documents and local installation arrangement.

1. Panel product warranty: defects in the equipment

A panel product warranty generally addresses defects in materials or manufacturing. The manufacturer's document controls what counts as a covered defect, how long coverage lasts, what remedy is offered and what conditions can void or limit coverage.

Read the exclusions rather than relying on a sales summary. Check how the warranty treats improper handling, unauthorized modification, unsuitable installation, environmental damage and failure to follow maintenance or operating instructions. The wording varies by manufacturer and product line.

Also check whether the remedy is repair, replacement with a comparable product, a prorated payment or another option. A replacement module may not be identical to the original model if that product is no longer made. Ask how compatibility with the existing array will be handled.

2. Panel performance warranty: a production threshold, not a savings promise

A performance warranty usually specifies a minimum level of panel power output over a stated period. It is separate from the product warranty: a panel can still produce electricity while its measured output falls below the warranty's stated threshold, or it can suffer a defect covered by the product warranty.

Look for the initial rated output, the permitted degradation schedule, the final-year threshold and the test method. The warranty may specify a claim threshold and require measurements under defined conditions. It does not automatically guarantee your home's annual electricity production, your utility-bill savings or a particular payback period.

Illustrative example, not a warranty offer: If a hypothetical panel starts at 400 watts and a document promises at least 85% of its original rated output at a specified later date, that threshold would be 340 watts under the document's measurement rules. The example explains the arithmetic only. Actual warranty schedules, measurement conditions and remedies differ.

Actual system output also depends on sunlight, temperature, shading, orientation, inverter operation, downtime, soiling and other site conditions. A performance warranty is not a substitute for checking the system's production history and diagnosing a fault.

3. Inverter and battery warranties: check the equipment separately

Panels are not the only components that can need attention. The inverter is essential to system operation, and its warranty may have a different term and claim process from the panel warranty. The U.S. Department of Energy notes that string inverters commonly last around 10 to 15 years, meaning one may need replacement during the life of a longer-lived panel array. That is a general lifespan observation, not a promise about a particular model.

For batteries, check both the product coverage and any capacity-retention or energy-throughput conditions. A document may set limits based on years, cycles, total energy discharged, operating temperature or other conditions. Do not compare two battery warranties by the year count alone.

Record the exact manufacturer, model and serial number for every major component. A proposal that says “premium inverter” or “long-life battery” is not enough to identify the warranty you are actually receiving.

4. Workmanship warranty: who stands behind the installation?

The installer's workmanship warranty is separate from the manufacturer's equipment warranties. It may cover certain installation defects, such as problems caused by the installer's work, but its scope and exclusions are contract-specific.

Pay particular attention to roof penetrations, flashing, mounting attachments, wiring, conduit and electrical connections. Ask who investigates a leak near a panel mount, who decides whether it is related to the solar installation and who pays for the repair if the claim is accepted. Do not assume the roofing manufacturer's warranty, roofer's workmanship warranty and solar installer's workmanship warranty automatically cover the same event.

Get the claim contact, response process, covered work and exclusions in writing. If a different contractor later removes and reinstalls the array for roof work, ask how that affects each applicable warranty before authorizing the work. The cost gap: a covered part does not always mean a free repair

This is where a warranty headline can create the wrong expectation. A manufacturer may agree that a component is defective and provide a replacement, while the homeowner still has to pay some costs associated with getting the system working again.

Depending on the warranty and service arrangement, possible costs include:

  • Travel, troubleshooting and diagnostic time.
  • Electrical disconnection and safe removal of the failed component.
  • Shipping, handling or return freight.
  • Labor to install the replacement, reconnect the system and test it.
  • Temporary access equipment or other site-specific work.
  • Roof repair or water-damage work if the issue falls outside the applicable coverage.

These are possible exclusions or separate charges, not a claim that every manufacturer excludes them. Some products or installer agreements include labor or service support. The only safe answer is the exact written term for the equipment and project you are buying.

Pro Tip: Ask this exact question before signing: “If this inverter or panel fails in year eight and the manufacturer approves the replacement, who pays for diagnosis, shipping, removal, installation, reconnection and testing?” Get a written answer that names the party responsible for each cost.

What happens if the solar installer goes out of business?

The installer closing does not automatically mean every manufacturer warranty disappears. A manufacturer's warranty may continue according to its own terms, but that does not guarantee that a local company will handle the diagnosis, paperwork, removal or replacement labor at no charge.

You may need to find another qualified solar service provider. That company may charge to inspect the system, identify the failed component, prepare a manufacturer claim or carry out the work. The manufacturer may also require specific evidence, an authorized process or proof of original purchase and installation.

Before signing, ask whether the installer provides a transferable workmanship warranty, whether a third-party service company supports the system and how a future owner can obtain the warranty documents. Do not treat a salesperson's assurance that “the manufacturer covers everything” as a complete service plan.

How to make a solar warranty claim

  1. Identify the fault. Save monitoring alerts, inverter error codes, photos and the date the issue began. Do not open electrical equipment or attempt repairs yourself.
  2. Find the responsible warranty. Check whether the issue concerns a panel, inverter, battery, installation work or roof interface. More than one party may need to be contacted.
  3. Gather proof. Locate the signed contract, invoices, equipment model and serial numbers, commissioning records, installation date, warranty certificates and maintenance history.
  4. Contact the named provider. Follow the warranty's claim instructions and keep copies of messages, case numbers, requested evidence and decisions.
  5. Confirm costs before authorizing work. Ask who pays for inspection, removal, shipping, replacement labor, reconnection and testing. Get the service scope and price in writing.
  6. Keep the closeout record. Save the repair report, replaced component details, updated serial numbers and proof that monitoring and system operation returned to normal.

If the system is unsafe, damaged or showing an electrical fault, stop using the affected equipment and contact a qualified professional. Warranty paperwork is not a reason to delay a safety inspection.

Keep the warranty documents with your home records, not only in an installer's customer portal. Save copies you can access if the company changes ownership, closes or removes your online account.

What homeowners report about warranty service

In one r/solar discussion, a homeowner described paying for troubleshooting and labor after a relatively new system needed a connector replacement. Other participants described different experiences with manufacturer support. Those comments illustrate why the labor question matters, but they are individual reports, not a measure of typical warranty costs or service quality. Your written warranty and local service options matter more than a stranger's claim story. Read the homeowner warranty discussion.

My take: The length of the warranty is the least useful number if you do not know how to make a claim and who pays to do the work. Compare the actual remedy, labor coverage, exclusions and service route. A shorter warranty with a clear local service obligation can be more useful than a longer headline promise that leaves you arranging and paying for the repair.

Frequently asked questions

Do solar panels really have a 25-year warranty?

Some panel products include long product and performance warranty terms, but there is no single universal 25-year warranty covering the entire solar system. Check the exact panel model documents and separate warranties for the inverter, battery and installation work.

Does a 25-year performance warranty guarantee my electricity bill savings?

No. It addresses panel output under the warranty's stated measurement rules. Household savings also depend on system design, sunlight, electricity use, utility rates, export compensation, equipment operation and other factors.

Are solar inverters covered for as long as the panels?

Not necessarily. Inverter warranty terms differ from panel terms, and string inverters commonly have a shorter service life than panels. Check the exact inverter model's warranty and the cost of future service.

Does the manufacturer pay for labor when a component fails?

It depends on the specific warranty and any separate labor or service agreement. Do not assume a replacement part includes diagnosis, shipping, removal, installation and testing.

What if my solar installer goes out of business?

A manufacturer warranty may still apply under its own terms, but you may need another qualified service provider and could face inspection or labor charges. Keep your contract, invoices, serial numbers and warranty documents.

Can solar warranties transfer when I sell my house?

Some warranties can transfer, but requirements vary. Check whether notice, registration, fees or other steps are required and complete them before closing.
