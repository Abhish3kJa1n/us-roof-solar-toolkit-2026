Solar Panel Installation Cost in 2026: What a Home System Costs

The useful starting point is the installed cash price per DC watt—not a hardware-only panel price, a monthly loan payment or a quote that quietly bundles a battery. Here is the latest published U.S. reference, what it does and does not tell you, and a simple way to check a written proposal.

2026 solar installation price per watt

EnergySage's 23rd Home Electrification Marketplace Report, covering January through June 2026, reported a median solar-only price of $2.62/W before incentives. Its overall median solar price across the broader mix of system types was $2.57/W. The solar-only figure is the more relevant comparison for a homeowner evaluating a photovoltaic system without a battery. EnergySage says its marketplace dataset draws on millions of homeowner shopping transactions and records quotes, equipment selections and financing choices. It is still marketplace data, not a census of every U.S. installation or a verified final invoice for every project. EnergySage's separate consumer cost page also displays about $2.60/W as an average; its H1 report labels $2.62/W as the solar-only median. Average and median are different statistics, so this guide does not merge them.

These are simple multiplication examples using one published marketplace median. They are not quoted price bands, local estimates, typical project totals or promises that an installer will offer that rate. A small, complex roof can price differently from a large, straightforward system; equipment, electrical work, roof work and regional labor also change the total.

Separate DOE benchmark—not a second consumer quote average.

The U.S. Department of Energy's 2025Q1 cost model lists a representative 8 kW DC residential rooftop PV-only system at a modeled market price of $2.95/W DC and a minimum sustainable price of $2.78/W DC. The DOE says its benchmark is built from national-laboratory analysis of industry cost data, with 2024 U.S. dollars used in its summary tables. This is a modeled system-cost benchmark, not the same dataset or measurement as EnergySage's marketplace median. Do not average the two figures or treat their difference as evidence that one source is wrong.

Source: U.S. Department of Energy — Solar Photovoltaic System Cost Benchmarks.

What a solar quote should separate before you compare price per watt

Use the PV system's DC nameplate capacity for a price-per-watt comparison, and use the written cash price for the same scope. Do not compare a PV-only cash quote with a battery-inclusive financed proposal or a monthly payment. Ask the installer to identify the following items in writing:

Quote itemWhat to recordWhy it must be separated
PV-only systemDC kW, module and inverter models, racking, installation labor, permits and interconnectionThis is the comparable base for a PV-only installed cash price per DC watt.
Battery storageBattery model, usable kWh, continuous power kW, backup equipment and installationStorage changes system scope and price; do not hide it inside a PV-only comparison.
Electrical or service workMain-panel/service upgrade, subpanel, trenching, wiring or other site workThese may be necessary for a particular home but are not the same thing as the PV array price.
Roof work and detach/resetRoof repairs, panel removal/reinstallation, attachment flashing and responsibility for leaksKeep roofing work and solar-system work visible, especially when both projects are being coordinated.
FinancingCash price, amount financed, APR, term, dealer/program fees, total payments and any required prepaymentA low payment or advertised APR can conceal a higher financed principal or payment change.
Utility and production assumptionsUtility rate plan, export credit, modeled annual kWh, equipment, roof planes and shade assumptionsSystem production and financial value depend on different assumptions; neither is established by price per watt alone.

The DOE's 2025Q1 residential benchmark makes the scope distinction concrete: its modeled market price was $2.95/W DC for an 8 kW PV-only system, versus $4.59/W DC for an 8 kW PV system paired with a 13.5 kWh battery. Those are different representative systems, not two price quotes for the same package. Both figures are in 2024 U.S. dollars. See the DOE benchmark tables and system definitions.

Cash price versus loan principal: check the actual amount owed

In its solar-financing report, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau illustrated a $30,000 cash project with a $9,000 hidden fee, producing a $39,000 loan principal before interest. That is an example in the CFPB report, not a claim that every solar loan has a fee. If the same example were an 8 kW DC PV system, the arithmetic would be $3.75/W DC at the cash price and $4.875/W DC at the loan principal. The difference is meaningful even before interest. Ask for the cash price and complete payment schedule for identical equipment and work scope; do not compare a quoted monthly payment with a cash benchmark. CFPB: Solar Financing.

2026 tax-credit check: the IRS states that the homeowner Residential Clean Energy Credit under Section 25D is not available for expenditures made after December 31, 2025. Do not treat an assumed future homeowner tax credit as cash already available to reduce the loan principal. For an individual project, timing and eligibility need to be checked against the current IRS Section 25D FAQs. This is the federal homeowner credit only; state, local and utility programs are separate and must be checked with the relevant authority.

Questions to resolve before signing

  • Is the quoted system size DC or AC, and is the price divided by DC nameplate watts?
  • Does the written cash price include permits, interconnection, racking, installation and required electrical work?
  • Are battery, roof work, service-panel upgrades and financing fees itemized separately?
  • What production estimate, roof-plane layout, shading assumptions and utility export rate support the savings claim?
  • What happens if the utility requires additional work, the roof needs repair, or the system does not receive permission to operate on the proposed schedule?

A benchmark is a comparison point, not a verdict on a particular quote. Local labor, roof complexity, equipment, electrical scope, utility requirements, storage and financing can change the total. This page does not rate an installer or determine whether a contract is fair.

Check the price per watt on a written solar quote

Enter the comparable PV-only installed cash price and the system's DC nameplate capacity. Do not include a battery, roof replacement, unrelated electrical work or financing charges in the PV-only figure. The quote should include the necessary photovoltaic equipment, mounting, installation labor, permits and interconnection work. If the proposal bundles several project types and does not break them out, ask for an itemized price before comparing.
