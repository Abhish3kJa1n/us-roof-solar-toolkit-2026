Metal roofing cost guide

Metal Roof Cost in 2026: Prices by Type, Size and System

Metal roof pricing is not one number. Steel, aluminum, copper and zinc differ, as do corrugated panels, metal shingles and standing-seam systems. This guide compares published estimates without blending different publishers' datasets or presenting them as a quote for your home.

What does a metal roof cost?

Two major consumer cost publishers report materially different national estimates. Their figures are broad planning references, not a controlled comparison of identical roofs. Roof area, system type, metal, gauge, fastening, roof geometry, tear-off, underlayment and local labor can change the price substantially.

Example: what the published range means for roof size

Using only HomeGuide's overall $5–$16 installed range, a measured 2,000-sq.-ft. roof produces a simple arithmetic interval of $10,000–$32,000. This illustrates how wide the planning interval is before considering property-specific roof complexity or checking the exact system. It is not a local estimate, an offer, a guaranteed range or a substitute for bids.

Be precise about the unit: a roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface. A home's advertised floor area is not the same measurement. Roof pitch, overhangs, dormers, valleys, porches and attached garages can make roof surface area materially different from living area.

What makes a metal roof quote increase?

  • Metal and coating: steel, aluminum, copper and zinc have different material costs; coating and finish are product-specific.
  • Panel profile and fastening: standing seam, exposed-fastener panels and metal shingles use different components and labor methods.
  • Gauge and panel specification: compare actual thickness/gauge and manufacturer specifications, not a vague “heavy-duty metal” description.
  • Roof geometry and pitch: valleys, hips, dormers, penetrations, steep slopes and limited access can add fabrication and labor.
  • Removal and disposal: confirm whether existing roofing, old fasteners and debris removal are included. Overlay rules depend on the system, existing roof and local requirements.
  • Underlayment, flashing and decking: ask what is replaced, where it is installed and how concealed damage is priced.
  • Trim and accessories: eaves, rake trim, ridge caps, valleys, snow retention, gutters and penetrations need defined scope.
  • Permits, cleanup and warranty: confirm permit responsibility, site protection, cleanup, manufacturer coverage and separate workmanship terms.

Is standing seam worth the extra cost?

Standing seam commonly has a higher quoted price than simpler exposed-fastener panel systems, but a price premium alone does not establish better value for every home. Compare the full system: metal, panel thickness, clip/fastener design, substrate, installation details, warranty and roof design. Angi's October 20, 2026 standing-seam guide reports a $9,400–$32,600 installed project range and a $19,000 average; those are publisher-level figures, not a local rate for your roof.

Frequently asked questions

Is a metal roof cheaper than shingles?

Common asphalt-shingle systems usually have a lower initial installed price than many metal systems, but the comparison depends on exact material, roof geometry, tear-off and included scope. Compare written, like-for-like proposals.

How much does a 2,000-square-foot metal roof cost?

HomeGuide's overall $5–$16 installed range implies $10,000–$32,000 for 2,000 square feet of roof surface by simple multiplication. This is not a property-specific estimate. Angi's broader metal range is $4–$30 per square foot, demonstrating that system and material selection materially change a planning number.

What is the cheapest type of metal roofing?

Published cost guides often place basic corrugated or exposed-fastener steel among lower-cost metal systems. The lowest bid is not necessarily the lowest ownership cost if fasteners, coatings, underlayment, flashing or workmanship differ.

Should I replace my roof before installing solar panels?

Not automatically. Compare the roof's documented condition and remaining service life with the solar system's expected service horizon, plus the actual cost of removing and reinstalling panels later. See should you replace a roof before solar?.
