Step 2 · PV system assumptions

Describe the solar array

These are editable model assumptions, not facts inferred from your city. The starting scenario uses a 1 kW DC reference system, 20° tilt, south-facing azimuth and 14% losses so the result can be read as kWh per kW DC. Replace these values with a proposal or roof-plane assumptions when known.

The 1 kW starting value is a reference configuration for per-kW yield, not a recommended home system size. Residential scope limit: 100 kW DC.
0° is horizontal; 90° is vertical. Do not assume roof pitch equals panel tilt on every installation.
PVWatts convention: 180° is south; 90° east; 270° west; 0° north.
Use a proposal/model value if supplied. Losses can include soiling, shading, wiring, mismatch, availability and other factors. They are not inspected here.

Waiting for location and inputs

Find a city, choose a returned Census place, and enter the array assumptions to request a typical-year model.

Model: National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) PVWatts V8. The model is driven by typical meteorological year weather data where available. Step 3 · Utility tariff (optional)

Look for nearby residential tariff records

OpenEI can return residential utility-rate records near the selected place when the site owner has configured its free server-side API key. If that key is missing, the lookup returns a configuration notice. Returned records are candidates only. City-centroid proximity does not establish which utility serves your property or which rate plan is on your bill. A separate state-average residential electricity-price benchmark is shown after place selection; it is not your marginal rate or a substitute for your bill.

Do not use a candidate tariff in a savings calculation until the utility, customer class, effective dates, import charges, fixed charges, export compensation and rate-plan name match your current utility bill and official tariff document.

What this tool knows—and what it does not

The city lookup returns a Census representative point for an incorporated place or Census-designated place. PVWatts estimates output using those coordinates and the system assumptions you enter. It does not know the exact roof location, slope, azimuth, obstructions, tree shade, structural capacity, fire setbacks or equipment selected by an installer.

Why this is not a savings estimate

Energy produced is not the same as money saved. The dollar value depends on when the household uses electricity, its marginal retail rate, the applicable export credit, tariff design, fixed charges, minimum bills, demand charges, interconnection rules and other factors. This page models production only; the related savings calculator requires the owner to enter their tariff and annual energy-flow assumptions.

Data quality and freshness

City place records are supplied by the US Census Bureau TIGERweb ACS2026 (January 1, 2026 vintage) service. City and Census-designated-place boundaries are statistical/geographic definitions, not necessarily utility territories. Solar output is returned by the NLR PVWatts V8 API. The model's climate dataset is a typical-year dataset, not live weather. The result records the model response date and any warnings returned by PVWatts.

Important limitations

A city is not an address lookup. It does not identify utility accounts, electricity rate plans, roof pitch, orientation, shading, permitting rules or local contractor prices.
