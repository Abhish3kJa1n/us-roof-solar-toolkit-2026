Assumptions and limitations

Annual production uses a PVWatts typical-year model, not a guarantee for a particular year. Weather and site conditions affect actual output; this calculator does not produce a location-specific multi-year range. See NLR guidance on interpreting PVWatts results.

This is an annual scenario, not a utility-bill simulator. It excludes fixed charges, taxes, demand charges, time-of-use periods, batteries, export caps, curtailment, shading changes, degradation, maintenance, inverter replacement, insurance, incentives, financing fees and future rate changes. The entered self-use share is an assumption, not a measured load profile. A blank export credit is unknown, not zero.

Federal tax-credit timing: the IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit under Section 25D is not available for expenditures made after December 31, 2025. This calculator does not include a federal tax credit. State, local and utility programs may differ; verify current eligibility with the relevant authority. IRS: Residential Clean Energy Credit.

How the annual solar-value model works

The calculator starts with annual electricity use in kWh, then sets a target for annual solar generation as a percentage of that use. It divides the target generation by the visitor-entered annual production per DC kW to estimate system size. System sizing can be shown without a self-use share or tariff. To estimate annual dollar value, enter a modeled annual direct-use share and the marginal import rate; if the system is expected to export electricity, also enter the applicable export credit. Unknown export compensation is not silently assumed to be zero. These are annual scenario calculations, not an hourly dispatch or utility billing simulation.

Why a solar kWh may not equal a retail kWh

Electric bills can include fixed customer charges, demand charges, time-of-use rates, minimum bills, taxes and other items that a solar array may not eliminate. Electricity exported to the grid may be credited differently from electricity consumed inside the home. The exact result depends on the utility tariff, service territory, system size, interconnection rules and when electricity is produced and consumed.

Inputs that matter most

  • 12 months of actual electricity usage in kWh, rather than bill dollars alone.
  • The current utility tariff, including time-of-use periods and export compensation.
  • Location-specific modeled production using roof orientation, tilt, shading and system losses.
  • Installed price, financing fees, equipment warranties, maintenance and any battery costs.
  • Current incentive eligibility and the homeowner's specific tax circumstances, verified independently.

Interpreting payback

The simple payback shown here is calculated only when the visitor supplies a complete energy-value model and an installed price. It divides the visitor-entered modeled installed price by the first-year modeled annual energy value. It is not a discounted cash-flow analysis and does not account for degradation, maintenance, inverter replacement, financing, taxes, future utility-rate changes, insurance or opportunity cost. A lender payment can also be higher than modeled savings, especially when financing fees or escalators are present.

What to do before making a decision

Compare the calculator with a proposal that includes an annual production estimate, equipment specifications, shade analysis, utility bill model, export-rate assumptions, total cash price and all financing terms. Confirm the utility's current rules and ask how the system performs under your actual rate plan.
