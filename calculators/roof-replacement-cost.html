What may not be included in the installed rate?

Check whether your proposal includes tear-off and disposal, underlayment, flashing, decking, ventilation, permits, steep-access charges, gutters and code upgrades. The rate you enter should match the scope you want to estimate.

Visitors may deliberately load the broad national reference; choosing Metal clears that general reference; select one published style or base-metal category to load its matching reference.

What this roof replacement estimate includes

The calculator multiplies measured roof surface area by the low and high installed-price assumptions. No extra pitch, complexity or contingency adjustment is added. The rate you enter must already reflect the selected material, access, tear-off, flashing, underlayment, decking assumptions, permits and other items included in your intended scope. This is transparent arithmetic, not a national price database. The material selector is descriptive. For metal, the metal selector automatically pre-fills one broad HomeGuide installed-rate category—either by style/system or by base metal; it remains editable and does not combine both dimensions.

Published national cost references — not local quotes.

Angi's cost guide, updated October 22, 2026, reports a broad $4–$11 per roof square foot range and a $9,610 national average based on 32,999 Angi Pro projects. Its article also says roof size, material, pitch and location affect the total. The broad range is prefilled as an editable national planning reference, not a local price or project quote.

HomeGuide separately reports a $5,700–$16,000 average total-cost range and a $10,800 national average, based on project costs reported by HomeGuide members. These publisher datasets use different methods and should not be treated as a statistically combined estimate.

Neither source provides a price for your home, city, contractor, roof geometry or exact material/scope. Do not assume the broad Angi range applies to a premium metal, tile, slate or complex low-slope project. Prefer current, comparable written local proposals for a real budget.

Historical Census survey context — not a quote or price per square foot.

The U.S. Census Bureau and HUD's 2023 American Housing Survey reported a median roofing expenditure of $10,000 for the 2021–2023 reference period, with approximately 8.3 million roofing projects and $93.5 billion in total roofing expenditure. The survey covers varied roofing improvement scopes; it does not establish that every project was a complete tear-off replacement.

Why roof size is not the same as home size

Roofing is generally priced from roof surface and project scope, not simply from the finished living area shown in a real-estate listing. A home with multiple stories can have a smaller footprint than its total interior square footage. Roof pitch, overhangs, attached garages, dormers, hips, valleys and additions also change the surface area.

Cost items to check in a written proposal

  • Removal and disposal of existing roofing layers, including any extra tear-off requirements.
  • Underlayment, ice-and-water protection where required, drip edge, flashing and ventilation.
  • Decking inspection and a clear unit price for replacement of damaged sheathing.
  • Access, steep-slope work, staging, gutters, skylights, chimney details and other penetrations.
  • Permits, code upgrades, cleanup, warranty terms, payment schedule and exclusions.

How to improve the estimate

Use a measured roof-surface area rather than a rough living-area proxy. Request comparable written scopes from properly licensed and insured contractors where required by your state or locality. Compare what is included, not just the total. Verify license status through the relevant state or local authority and check insurance documentation directly.

How should you use the result?

Use the low and high totals to frame a planning conversation and identify questions. Do not treat the range as a quote, insurance settlement value, appraisal, or prediction of what a particular contractor will charge. If the roof has leaks, sagging, widespread damage or suspected structural problems, arrange an inspection rather than relying on a cost calculator.
