    Frequently asked questions

    Common roof and solar planning questions.

    General educational information. Always check your roof, local market, electric tariff and current program rules independently.

    How do I know when my roof needs to be replaced?
    Age is only one clue. Leaks, missing or damaged shingles, soft decking, recurring repairs, ventilation issues and storm damage can all affect the decision. This calculator uses only the condition you report to suggest inspection or monitoring; it does not calculate remaining roof life or decide whether replacement is necessary. Roof age is displayed as context only. Ask a qualified local roofing professional to inspect the roof.
    Can solar panels be installed on any roof?
    No. Roof condition, remaining useful life, orientation, slope, shading, obstructions, structural capacity, fire setbacks, local code and utility requirements all matter. A roof that needs replacement may be better addressed before solar installation. A solar professional and, when appropriate, a structural professional should verify suitability.
    How is the solar savings estimate calculated?
    The calculator requires annual kWh from utility bills. It uses a visitor-entered import rate to value directly used solar electricity, and a separately entered export rate to value surplus generation. Dollar value is not calculated without an import rate. It values self-consumed generation at that rate and exported generation at the separately entered export-compensation rate. Actual savings depend on hourly load, system design, weather, shading, tariffs, fixed charges and utility export rules.
    Does the estimate include tax credits or local incentives?
    No incentives, rebates or tax credits are automatically included. Eligibility and value depend on current federal, state, local and utility rules, project ownership, timing and individual tax circumstances. Verify current programs with official government or utility sources and consult a qualified tax professional. Do not rely on this calculator for tax advice.
    What does the financing estimate mean?
    The optional loan figure is a mathematical payment illustration based on the system-cost assumption, APR and term entered. It is not an offer, prequalification, lender quote or approval. Real financing may include fees, dealer charges, escalators, different terms, liens or other contract provisions that are not modeled here.
    How accurate are these estimates?
    They are early planning ranges, not measurements or quotes. Roof dimensions, material and labor pricing, access, tear-off layers, decking condition, permits, waste, local code, solar orientation, shade, structural capacity, production model, utility rules and incentives can materially change the outcome. A site inspection, professional measurement and written proposal are required.
    Federal homeowner solar tax credit in 2026: As of October 31, 2026, the IRS Section 25D law-change FAQ states that the homeowner Residential Clean Energy Credit is not allowed for expenditures after December 31, 2025. For an individual item, the expenditure is treated as made when its original installation is completed; paying on or before December 31, 2025 does not preserve the credit if installation is completed after that date. The FAQ states a separate timing rule for expenditures connected with construction or reconstruction of a structure. This is general information about homeowner Section 25D, not a determination of an individual's eligibility. This calculator does not model tax credits, leases, power-purchase agreements or provider-level tax treatment. Verify current rules with the IRS and a qualified tax professional. Read the IRS law-change FAQs for the current Section 25D timing rule. State, local and utility programs are separate and may change.
