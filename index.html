    Using your estimate

    Get a useful starting point. Then check the details.

    A calculator is only as good as the measurements and assumptions behind it. These three steps will help you avoid treating a rough estimate like a contractor quote.

    01

    Start with measurements and bills

    Use measured roof area if you have it. For electricity, use the annual kWh from your utility bills rather than working backwards from the amount you pay each month.

    02

    Use assumptions you can explain

    Compare roof prices that cover the same work. For solar, use a location-specific production estimate and check your utility import rate and export credit. Leave figures blank when you do not know them.

    03

    Check the actual project

    Have the roof measured and inspected. Ask contractors to spell out materials, removal, flashing, ventilation and other work. For solar, check the system design, utility tariff and written proposal before signing.

    The numbers, step by step

    See what goes into an estimate.

    These examples use made-up inputs to show the arithmetic. They are not typical project prices, local market estimates or promises about what a home will save.

    Example 1 · Roof replacement

    How a roof-cost range is calculated

    Say a measured roof has 1,800 square feet of surface area. A homeowner is comparing two written estimates that work out to $5.50 and $8.00 per roof square foot.

    Measured roof area1,800 sq ft
    Lower rate assumption$5.50 / sq ft
    Higher rate assumption$8.00 / sq ft
    Illustrative range$9,900–$14,400

    That is multiplication, not a quote. The result is only useful if both rates cover comparable work. Tear-off, disposal, underlayment, flashing, decking repairs, ventilation, permits and cleanup can change the final price. The calculator does not add those items automatically.

    Example 2 · Solar energy value

    Why production and bill savings are different

    Suppose a household uses 10,800 kWh a year and wants to model solar generation equal to 80% of that use. The production assumption is 1,350 kWh per installed DC kW each year.

    Generation target10,800 × 80% = 8,640 kWh
    Estimated system size8,640 ÷ 1,350 = 6.4 kW
    Used directly (40%)3,456 kWh
    Sent to the grid5,184 kWh
    Direct-use value at $0.18/kWh$622.08
    Export value at $0.05/kWh$259.20
    Modeled annual energy value$881.28

    The $881.28 is a modeled value for these assumptions, not a guaranteed reduction in the utility bill. Actual results depend on when the home uses power, the system's real production, the utility's export rules and charges that solar does not remove.

    Where to check the inputs

    Use a roof measurement and comparable written bids for a real roofing project. For solar production, run the property's location and system assumptions through PVWatts. It models expected production from location, weather and system inputs; it does not inspect a roof, confirm shade on your property or predict your utility bill. Your utility bill and current tariff are the right starting points for import rates and export credits.

    Each source answers a different question. PVWatts is a production model. The Census Bureau and HUD provide historical national home-improvement spending context. Your written roofing bids establish the scope and price of a specific job, while your utility's current tariff determines how imported and exported electricity are valued. None of these sources can stand in for the others.

    The U.S. Census Bureau and HUD's 2023 Home Improvements infographic reports a $10,000 median roofing expenditure for its 2021–2023 reference period. That is national survey context, not a current price per square foot and not an input to this calculator.

    Before you rely on a number

    Two details can change an estimate quite a bit.

    A couple of common assumptions can make a roof or solar estimate look more certain than it really is.

    Your home's square footage is not its roof area

    A 2,000-square-foot home does not automatically have a 2,000-square-foot roof. Roof surface area depends on the building footprint, number of stories, pitch, overhangs and layout. Use a measured roof area when you have one. A home-area calculation is only a fallback for early planning.

    Estimate roof area

    Solar production is not the same as bill savings

    Solar panels can generate electricity when your home is using very little power. Extra electricity may go to the grid, and your utility may credit it at a different rate from the price you pay. Fixed charges may remain. Matching 80% of annual use with solar generation does not automatically cut your total bill by 80%.

    Frequently asked questions

    Common roof and solar planning questions.

    General educational information. Always check your roof, local market, electric tariff and current program rules independently.

    How do I know when my roof needs to be replaced?
    Age is only one clue. Leaks, missing or damaged shingles, soft decking, recurring repairs, ventilation issues and storm damage can all affect the decision. This calculator uses only the condition you report to suggest inspection or monitoring; it does not calculate remaining roof life or decide whether replacement is necessary. Roof age is displayed as context only. Ask a qualified local roofing professional to inspect the roof.
    Can solar panels be installed on any roof?
    No. Roof condition, remaining useful life, orientation, slope, shading, obstructions, structural capacity, fire setbacks, local code and utility requirements all matter. A roof that needs replacement may be better addressed before solar installation. A solar professional and, when appropriate, a structural professional should verify suitability.
    How should I compare roof replacement bids?
    Start with the same measured roof area, roofing product and work scope. Check whether each price includes tear-off and disposal, underlayment, flashing, drip edge, ventilation, permits, cleanup and the same warranty coverage. Compare the decking-repair allowance and the price for extra work, too. Ask each contractor to put exclusions, payment milestones and change-order terms in writing. A lower total may leave more work outside the contract. Use the three-bid comparison worksheet to line up the details.
    How is the solar savings estimate calculated?
    The calculator requires annual kWh from utility bills. It uses a visitor-entered import rate to value directly used solar electricity, and a separately entered export rate to value surplus generation. Dollar value is not calculated without an import rate. It values self-consumed generation at that rate and exported generation at the separately entered export-compensation rate. Actual savings depend on hourly load, system design, weather, shading, tariffs, fixed charges and utility export rules.
    Does the estimate include tax credits or local incentives?
    No incentives, rebates or tax credits are automatically included. Eligibility and value depend on current federal, state, local and utility rules, project ownership, timing and individual tax circumstances. Verify current programs with official government or utility sources and consult a qualified tax professional. Do not rely on this calculator for tax advice.
    What does the financing estimate mean?
    The optional loan figure is a mathematical payment illustration based on the system-cost assumption, APR and term entered. It is not an offer, prequalification, lender quote or approval. Real financing may include fees, dealer charges, escalators, different terms, liens or other contract provisions that are not modeled here.
    How accurate are these estimates?
    They are early planning ranges, not measurements or quotes. Roof dimensions, material and labor pricing, access, tear-off layers, decking condition, permits, waste, local code, solar orientation, shade, structural capacity, production model, utility rules and incentives can materially change the outcome. A site inspection, professional measurement and written proposal are required.
